# Knife Dance

> **Pitch (read aloud):** *Hand flat on the table, fingers spread. The drummer starts slow. The knife point stabs past your thumb, between each finger, past your little finger, and back, one stab on every beat. Then the drum gets faster. And faster.*

**Description:** A quarterly test of nerve. Dancers stab a knife between their spread fingers in time to a drum, and the drum speeds up with every **stage**. Each stage needs a harder **Sleight of Hand** check. Stop at any time, and the number of stages you've cleared becomes your score. If you **slip**, your score is only **half** your cleared stages. The highest score wins. **Real knife, bare hand:** a slip draws blood.

## How to Play

### The setup

- **One dedicated table.** Dancers go one at a time, in an order drawn by lot.
- **Every dancer gets their own knife.**
- **The dancer** lays one hand flat on the table with the fingers spread, and holds the knife in the other hand.
- **One stage** is one trip across the fingers and back, with one stab on every drumbeat.
- For each new stage, the drummer plays **faster**.

### Your turn, step by step

1. **Before each stage, choose:** **stop** (the number of stages you've cleared is your score), or **go on**.
2. **Go on:** make a **Dexterity (Sleight of Hand)** check at **DC 8 + 2 × the stage number**.

   | Stage | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | DC | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 | 18 | 20 | 22 | 24 |

   - **Success:** you've cleared that stage. Choose again.
   - **Failure:** you **slip**. The knife hits your hand: take **1d4 + the stage number** piercing damage. Your turn ends, and your score is **half** your cleared stages, rounded down.

### Injuries

- **No healers.** If you regain hit points before the winner is decided, you're out of the game and your score no longer counts.
- **Drop to 0 HP** and you're **stable**: you make no death saving throws.

### Winning

- Later dancers know the best score so far, and only need to beat it.
- The highest score wins.
- **Ties:** the tied dancers each try the **stage above the tied score**. A dancer who slips, or is at 0 HP, is out of the tie. If more than one is left, go up another stage. If nobody is left, they **split the prize**.

## Dice Examples

**Dancers** (in the order they drew): Mirela (PC, Sleight of Hand +2), Lark Thistlewhite (NPC, Sleight of Hand +5) and Quill (PC, Sleight of Hand +7).

**Mirela**
- Stage 1 (DC 10): **12** + 2 = **14**. ✓
- Stage 2 (DC 12): **11** + 2 = **13**. ✓
- Stage 3 (DC 14): **13** + 2 = **15**. ✓
- She **stops**. **Score: 3.**

**Lark** needs to beat 3.
- Stages 1–3: **10**, **9**, **10**, each + 5 = 15, 14, 15. ✓ ✓ ✓
- Stage 4 (DC 16): **7** + 5 = **12**. **Slip!** The knife hits: **3** + 4 = **7** piercing damage. His score is half of 3, rounded down: **1.**

**Quill** needs to beat 3.
- Stages 1–3: **13**, **8**, **9**, each + 7 = 20, 15, 16. ✓ ✓ ✓
- Stage 4 (DC 16): **11** + 7 = **18**. ✓
- He **stops**. **Score: 4. Quill wins.**

**Edge case: a tie**
- If Quill had stopped at 3, he and Mirela would both try stage 4 (DC 16). If Quill succeeds and Mirela slips, Quill wins. If both slip, they split the prize.

## Odds

**Chance to clear each stage:**

| Sleight of Hand | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| +2 | 65% | 55% | 45% | 35% | 25% | 15% | 5% | 0% |
| +5 | 80% | 70% | 60% | 50% | 40% | 30% | 20% | 10% |
| +7 | 90% | 80% | 70% | 60% | 50% | 40% | 30% | 20% |

**A slip hurts more the further you go:** 1d4 + the stage number averages **3.5** at stage 1, **6.5** at stage 4 and **10.5** at stage 8 (12 at most).

**Best place to stop** (if you just want the highest average score): stage 2 at +2, stage 3 at +5 and +7. But you only need to beat the best score so far.

## Rulings & Cheating

### Fair Play: what's allowed

- Choosing when to stop.
- Expertise and other natural bonuses.

### What counts as cheating

- **Any spell or magic**, such as *guidance*, *enhance ability*, *minor illusion* or Bardic Inspiration, and any magic item.

### How to cheat (and how you get caught)

Each cheat is one **contest against the Floor Watcher's Wisdom (Perception) +5**. **Ties go to the Watcher.** The party can help, but each person who tries a cheat makes their own roll and risks being caught.

| Cheat | Who can do it | Roll vs the Watcher | If it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| While the crowd watches the dancer before you, quietly cast *guidance* or *enhance ability* (Cat's Grace) on yourself | A dancer who can cast it | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | *Guidance*: add 1d4 to one stage. *Enhance ability*: advantage on every stage this turn |
| Hidden spell (*minor illusion*): the sound of a wasp at a rival's ear as their stage starts | A caster in the crowd, within 30 ft | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | Disadvantage on that stage |
| Bardic Inspiration, shouted as a cheer | A bard in the crowd, within 60 ft | Cha (Deception) to pass it off as ordinary crowd noise | The dancer adds the die to one stage |

### Cheating dice example

Mirela, watching from the crowd, shouts Bardic Inspiration to Quill as a cheer.
- Mirela: Deception **9** + 7 = **16**. The Watcher: **15** + 5 = **20**. **Caught.**
- Mirela is banned for 3 months and pinned to the notice board. To clear her name: 5 gp, 1 day serving, or wait 3 months.

## Hooks / NPCs

- **Bosun Harl** has bet the ship's purser 40 gp of the crew's shore pay that he'll clear stage 6 at next week's dance. Even a +7 hand does that only about 1 time in 16. The crew offer any caster in the party 10 gp to help him quietly. The purser offers 10 gp to put him off. Neither knows the other asked.
- **A butcher and his apprentice** have a deal: the first week the apprentice outscores him, he can marry the butcher's daughter. The apprentice has been trying for a year. She's tired of waiting, and she begs any caster in the party to slip him *guidance* with a pat on the back. He's proud, and he has told everyone he'd call off the wedding before he'd win by cheating.
