# Tall-Tale Tribunal

> **Pitch (read aloud):** *Three judges sit behind a long table in powdered wigs made of mop heads. You draw a card, read it in secret, and then you have one minute to tell the room about the time you met a monster. Was it true? The judges will decide.*

**Description:** A monthly storytelling contest. Each teller draws a card naming a monster and saying whether their tale must be **TRUE** or **FALSE**. They tell the tale, and three judges each decide whether they believe it. A teller scores **1 point for every judge who believes them**, whether the tale was true or not. The highest score wins the "Licensed Liar" sash.

## How to Play

### The judges

| Judge | Wisdom (Insight) |
|---|---|
| Judge Hollin | +3 |
| Madam Vesk | +5 |
| Old Rook | +7 |

### What the card means

- **TRUE:** tell something that really happened to you or someone you know. If you've never met this monster, tell what's really known about it, as though you'd seen it yourself.
- **FALSE:** make it up, as wild as you like.

### A teller's turn, step by step

Tellers go in the order they sign up.

1. **Draw a card.** Read it in secret. It names a monster and says **TRUE** or **FALSE**.
2. **Tell the tale** (about one minute; players are encouraged to actually tell it).
3. **Roll against each judge.** The teller makes a separate roll against each of the three judges:
   - **FALSE card:** the teller rolls **Charisma (Deception)**.
   - **TRUE card:** the teller rolls **Charisma (Persuasion)**. True stories still have to sound believable.

   Each judge rolls **Wisdom (Insight)**.
   - **The teller's total is higher:** that judge **believes** the tale. **1 point.**
   - **The judge's total is equal or higher:** that judge **doubts** it. No point. (**Ties go to the judge.**)
4. **Reveal the card** to the crowd.

### Winning

- The highest score wins.
- **Ties:** the tied tellers each roll **Charisma (Performance)** for the crowd's cheers. The highest total wins. If they tie again, they split the gold and take turns wearing the sash.
- **A teller who scores 0** has convinced nobody, and buys the three judges a mug of ale each.

## Dice Examples

**Tellers:** Mirela (PC, Deception +7, Persuasion +5, Performance +7) and Thokk (NPC, Deception −1, Persuasion −1, Performance −1).

**Mirela draws "Owlbear: FALSE"** and tells of the owlbear she taught to dance. She rolls Deception (+7) against each judge.
- vs Judge Hollin: **12** + 7 = **19**. Hollin: **14** + 3 = **17**. **Believes.** 1 point.
- vs Madam Vesk: **8** + 7 = **15**. Vesk: **10** + 5 = **15**. A tie goes to the judge. **Doubts.**
- vs Old Rook: **16** + 7 = **23**. Rook: **15** + 7 = **22**. **Believes.** 2 points.
- **Mirela scores 2.**

**Thokk draws "Rust monster: TRUE"**, and it really did eat his grandfather's axe. He rolls Persuasion (−1) against each judge.
- vs Hollin: **18** − 1 = **17**. Hollin: **9** + 3 = **12**. **Believes.** 1 point.
- vs Vesk: **7** − 1 = **6**. Vesk: **11** + 5 = **16**. **Doubts.**
- vs Rook: **13** − 1 = **12**. Rook: **14** + 7 = **21**. **Doubts.**
- **Thokk scores 1.**

**Mirela wins** the sash.

**Edge case: a tie**
- If Thokk had also scored 2, both would roll Performance: Mirela at +7 against Thokk at −1. The crowd almost always cheers for the bard.

## Prizes & Stakes

- **Entry:** 5 sp per teller.
- **Winner:** 4 gp and the **"Licensed Liar" sash**, worn until next month's Tribunal.
- **Scoring 0:** you buy the three judges a mug of ale each (1 sp 2 cp in all).
- **The House keeps:** all entries and the bar sales.

## Odds

**Chance to win against each judge, and to score 2 or more points:**

| Teller's bonus | vs Hollin (+3) | vs Vesk (+5) | vs Rook (+7) | 2+ points | Average points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| +4 | 52.5% | 43% | 34% | 40% | 1.3 |
| +6 | 62% | 52.5% | 43% | 53.5% | 1.6 |
| +8 | 70% | 62% | 52.5% | 67% | 1.8 |

**A teller who is better at lying than persuading** is happy to draw FALSE cards. A teller with the same bonus in both doesn't care.

## Rulings & Cheating

### Fair Play: what's allowed

- Any story, as wild as you like, as long as it's about the monster on your card.
- Props, sound effects and audience participation.
- Expertise and other natural bonuses.

### What counts as cheating

- **Any spell or magic**, such as *charm person*, *friends*, *enhance ability*, *suggestion*, or Bardic Inspiration from someone else.
- Swapping your card, bribing a judge, or heckling a rival on purpose.

### How to cheat (and how you get caught)

Each cheat is one **contest against the Floor Watcher's Wisdom (Perception) +5**. **Ties go to the Watcher.** The party can help, but each person who tries a cheat makes their own roll and risks being caught.

| Cheat | Who can do it | Roll vs the Watcher | If it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Swap your card for one up your sleeve | Teller | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | Choose TRUE or FALSE yourself |
| Bribe Judge Hollin or Madam Vesk (1 gp). Old Rook can't be bribed. | Anyone | Cha (Persuasion) | That judge believes you automatically |
| Hidden spell: *enhance ability* by touch before you start, or *friends* on one judge | The teller, or a friend with a pat on the back | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | *Enhance ability*: advantage on all three rolls. *Friends*: advantage against that judge, and the judge knows afterwards. |
| Hidden spell: *charm person* or *suggestion* on one judge | A caster in the crowd, or the teller | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | The judge makes a Wisdom save against your spell save DC, using their Insight bonus. On a failure, *charm person* gives advantage against that judge, and the judge knows afterwards. *Suggestion* makes that judge believe you automatically. |
| Bardic Inspiration, shouted as a cheer | A bard in the crowd, within 60 ft | Cha (Deception) to pass it off as ordinary crowd noise | The teller adds the die to one roll against a judge |
| Heckle a rival from the crowd | Anyone in the crowd | Cha (Deception) to look like an honest drunk | The rival has disadvantage against one judge |

### Caught cheating: penalties

The Tall-Tale Tribunal is a **public event**. Penalties apply to the cheater **and** to any accomplice who is caught. A bribed judge is replaced for a year.

![[Cheating Penalties#Public Events]]

| Fine | Or days serving the event |
|---|---|
| 5 gp | 1 day |

### Cheating dice example

Mirela slips Madam Vesk 1 gp before her tale.
- Mirela: Persuasion **6** + 5 = **11**. The Watcher: **9** + 5 = **14**. **Caught.**
- Mirela is disqualified, banned for 3 months, and her portrait goes up on the notice board, with "Not a Licensed Liar" scrawled underneath. To clear her name: 5 gp, 1 day serving, or wait 3 months.

## Hooks / NPCs

- **Old Rook** is blind in one eye and claims the other sees only lies. He has never been bribed. He has been threatened.
- **A card in the deck** names a monster nobody has heard of. This month a teller draws it with FALSE and makes up a tale, in detail. Next week, someone finds tracks.
