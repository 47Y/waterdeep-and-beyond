# Name That Monster

> **Pitch (read aloud):** *"Clue one," says the host, pausing for effect. "It has been seen in the deepest caves... and the highest towers." Silence. "Clue two: it does not need to blink." A hand shoots up. Is it brave, or foolish?*

**Description:** A monster quiz, played with **real player knowledge**. There are no dice and no DCs. The DM opens a monster book and reads clues about a monster, from the most obscure to the most obvious. Anyone can try to name it. **A right answer scores 1 point. A wrong answer loses 1 point**, and scores can go negative. Five monsters a night; the most points wins.

## How to Play

### Each monster, step by step

1. **The DM picks a monster** from a monster book and plays the host. In the tavern, the host is reading from a card.
2. **The DM reads clue 1**, one fact from the monster's entry. Clues go from the most obscure to the most obvious, with **up to five clues** per monster. Never read the name or show the picture.
3. **Anyone can call out a name.** There's no turn order and no roll. It's what the **player** knows that counts, not the character.
   - **Right:** that player scores **+1**, and the monster is done.
   - **Wrong:** that player scores **−1**, and can't guess again **on this clue**. Anyone else can still guess.
   - **Two players call out together:** the DM takes whoever they heard first. If that answer is wrong, the other player's answer still counts.
4. **When nobody else wants to guess,** the DM reads the next clue. Every player can guess again on the new clue, including anyone who was wrong before.
5. **If nobody has named it after the last clue,** the DM reveals the monster and nobody scores.

### Winning

- The **highest score after five monsters** wins, even if it's negative.
- **Ties:** one more monster, for the tied players only. Still tied: another monster.

## Example

**Players:** Mirela's, Quill's and Thokk's players. Everyone starts at **0**. The DM has opened the book to the beholder.

**Monster 1**
- **Clue 1:** "It has been seen in the deepest caves, and the highest towers."
  - Quill: "A mind flayer!" Wrong. **Quill −1.** He can't guess again on this clue.
  - Thokk: "A dragon!" Wrong. **Thokk −1.**
  - Mirela waits. Nobody else wants to guess, so the DM moves on.
- **Clue 2:** "It does not need to blink." Nobody guesses, so the DM moves on.
- **Clue 3:** "Its gaze can unmake magic."
  - Quill: "A gibbering mouther!" Wrong. **Quill −2.**
  - Mirela: "A beholder!" Right. **Mirela +1.** The monster is done.

**Scores after monster 1:** Mirela +1, Thokk −1, Quill −2.

**Edge case: everyone ends below zero**
- After five monsters, the scores are Thokk −1, Mirela −2 and Quill −4. **Thokk wins:** −1 is the highest score.

## Prizes & Stakes

- **Entry:** 5 sp per player.
- **Winner:** 5 gp from the Adventurers' Guild, which sponsors the night, plus a free drink.
- **The House keeps:** all entries and the bar sales.

## Odds

There are no dice, so there are no odds to work out. A right answer gains as much as a wrong one loses, so **a guess only pays if you're more likely to be right than wrong**. Waiting costs nothing, but someone else may name it first.

## Rulings & Cheating

### Fair Play: what's allowed

- Waiting as long as you like before guessing.
- Guessing early, at the risk of losing a point.
- Anything you already know about the monster.

### What counts as cheating

Looking it up: Google, a rulebook, or any other outside tool.

### What happens

**Cheating is at the DM's discretion.** There's no roll and no Floor Watcher here, because the player is the one cheating. If the DM suspects someone is looking up answers, the DM decides what happens.

A player can try to bribe the DM. It's probably not a good idea.

## Hooks / NPCs

- **Sage Odette** is a retired monster hunter who hosts the night. She reads every clue with a straight face. Except when the monster is a vampire.
- **The host's cards** are written by the Adventurers' Guild. One week, the card describes a monster nobody can name, and the Guild offers a reward for its name.
