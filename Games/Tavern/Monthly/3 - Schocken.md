# Schocken

> **Pitch (read aloud):** *Three dice in a leather cup. Slam it on the table, peek, keep what you like, roll the rest. Three ones is the best hand in the game, and the whole table groans when somebody gets it. The worst hand drinks.*

**Description:** A monthly dice game from the mountain taverns. Each player rolls **three six-sided dice up to three times**, keeping any dice they like between rolls. The best hand wins the pot and the worst hand drinks. The first player of each round, called the lead, decides how many rolls everyone else gets.

## How to Play

### Setting up

- **Every player has their own leather cup and three dice**, supplied by the House.
- Dice you **keep** go on the table beside your cup, face up, where everyone can see them.
- Your **last roll stays hidden under your cup** until everyone has gone. Only you peek at it.

### A round, step by step

1. **Everyone puts 1 sp** into the pot.
2. **The lead rolls.** Whoever paid first leads the first round, and the lead moves one seat to the left each round. The lead shakes all three dice in the cup, slams it upside down on the table and peeks under it. They may stop there. Or they may move any dice they want to keep beside the cup and roll the others the same way, **up to three rolls in all**. A die you've kept stays kept.
3. **The number of rolls the lead used** is the most anyone else may use this round. (If the lead stops after 1 roll, everyone gets only 1.) Everyone can see how many rolls that was, but not the dice still under the cup.
4. **Everyone else** takes their turn the same way, going left from the lead. You may stop early too.
5. **Everyone lifts their cup. The best hand wins the pot. The worst hand** pays 1 sp for one shot of schnapps and **drinks** it: one Constitution save, DC 10, on the Drunk Track.

Play as many rounds as you like, and leave between rounds whenever you want. A player who goes **Out** on the Drunk Track is done for the night.

### Hands, best to worst

| Rank | Hand | Example | Ranked within by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Schock Out**: three 1s | 1-1-1 | (Unbeatable) |
| 2 | **Schock**: two 1s and another die | 1-1-6 | The third die: 1-1-6 beats 1-1-2 |
| 3 | **General**: three of a kind | 4-4-4 | The number: 6-6-6 beats 2-2-2 |
| 4 | **Street**: three in a row | 3-4-5 | The highest die: 4-5-6 beats 1-2-3 |
| 5 | **Plain Number**: anything else | 6-5-2 | Read the dice high to low as a number: 652 beats 641 |

### Ties

- **Tied for best:** the player who used **fewer rolls** wins. If they used the same number of rolls, they split the pot.
- **Tied for worst:** the player who used **more rolls** drinks. If they used the same number of rolls, they both drink.

## Dice Examples

**Players:** Mirela (lead), Thokk and Quill. The pot is 3 sp.

**Mirela** (lead)
- Roll 1: **1, 4, 6**. She keeps the 1, where everyone can see it.
- Roll 2: **1, 5**. She now has **1-1-5: a Schock with a 5.** She stops after 2 rolls, with the 1 and 5 still under her cup.
- Everyone else may use **at most 2 rolls**.

**Thokk**
- Roll 1: **3, 3, 3**. **General 3.** He stops after 1 roll. The table has no idea what's under his cup, and it makes them nervous.

**Quill**
- Roll 1: **6, 5, 2**. He keeps the 6 and 5.
- Roll 2: **4**. He has **4-5-6: Street.** He's out of rolls.

**Everyone lifts.** Mirela's Schock beats Thokk's General 3, which beats Quill's Street. **Mirela wins 3 sp. Quill drinks.**

**Edge case: equal hands**
- If Thokk had also rolled 1-1-5 but needed 2 rolls, he and Mirela would tie on hands and rolls, and split the pot.

## Prizes & Stakes

- **Cost:** 1 sp per player, per round. The best hand takes the pot.
- **The worst hand** drinks, at 1 sp a shot.
- **The House keeps:** nothing from the pot, only the price of the shots.

## Odds

**On a single roll:**

| Hand | Chance |
|---|---|
| Schock Out (1-1-1) | 0.5% |
| Schock (two 1s) | 7% |
| General (three of a kind, not 1s) | 2.3% |
| Street | 11% |
| Plain Number | 79% |

**Chasing three 1s** over three rolls (keeping every 1) works about **7.5%** of the time. A lead who stops early on a good hand makes the whole table's job much harder.

## Rulings & Cheating

### Fair Play: what's allowed

- Keeping and rerolling any dice.
- Stopping early to limit everyone else's rolls, even on a bad hand.
- Bluffing, groaning and pretending a good hand is bad.

### What counts as cheating

- **Any spell or magic**, such as *minor illusion*.
- Loaded dice, palming a die, or turning a die over while lifting the cup.

### How to cheat (and how you get caught)

Each cheat is one **contest against the Floor Watcher's Wisdom (Perception) +5**. **Ties go to the Watcher.** The party can help, but each person who tries a cheat makes their own roll and risks being caught.

| Cheat | Who can do it | Roll vs the Watcher | If it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| As you lift the cup, turn one die under it to a 1, or swap it for a 1 palmed in your hand | Player | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | That die becomes a 1 |
| Swap the House's dice for your own loaded dice | Player | Dex (Sleight of Hand) to swap them in, and again each round you keep using them, because the 1s start to get noticed | Each die you roll shows a 1 on a 1 or 2 (roll 1d6: a 2 counts as a 1) |
| *Minor illusion* over a die as the cups lift | A caster at the table | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | One die looks like any face you choose, until someone picks it up |

### Caught cheating: penalties

Schocken is a **public event**. Penalties apply to the cheater **and** to any accomplice who is caught. A caught cheater forfeits any pot they won that round.

| Fine | Or days serving the event |

| 1 gp | 1 day |

### Cheating dice example

Quill has 1-2-6, with the 2 under his cup. He turns the 2 to a 1 as he lifts the cup, hoping for a Schock.
- Quill: Sleight of Hand **6** + 7 = **13**. The Watcher: **9** + 5 = **14**. **Caught.**
- Quill forfeits his 1 sp, is banned from the Schocken table for 3 months, and is pinned to the notice board. To clear his name: 1 gp, 1 day serving, or wait 3 months.

## Hooks / NPCs

- **One old leather cup** behind the bar is the one everyone asks for. Regulars swear it rolls more 1s for anyone with a death in the family.
- **A merchant** plays every month, always one-on-one with a stranger, and always loses on purpose. The silver is small. What matters is the folded note he leaves under his cup for the winner, right under the Watcher's nose.
