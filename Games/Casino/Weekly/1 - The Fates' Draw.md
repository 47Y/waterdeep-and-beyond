# The Fates' Draw

> **Pitch (read aloud):** *Once a tenday, the lamps dim and a masked figure turns three brass drums on the casino stage. Each drum spits out a single numbered stone. Somewhere in the crowd, a ticket holder is about to scream.*

**Description:** The House lottery. Players buy a ticket and pick **three digits** (0–9). At the end of each tenday the House draws three numbers on stage. Match all three and you win a fortune. Tickets are cheap and sold at the bar, the casino door and the arena gate, up until the draw.

## How to Play

### Buying a ticket

1. **Pick three digits**, each from 0 to 9. You can repeat digits (such as 7-7-2).
2. **Pick a ticket type:**
   - **Exact Order:** your digits must match the draw **in exact order**.
   - **Any Order:** your digits must match the draw **in any order**. This ticket must use three **different** digits.
3. **Pay** for the ticket. A ticket costs **1 sp, 1 gp or 1 pp**, and the payout scales with the price.
4. **Keep your ticket.** The clerk writes your digits on it and stamps it with the House seal. No ticket, no prize.

You can buy up to **10 tickets** for each draw. The House sells a number no more than **5 times at each price**, and after that the clerk says it's sold out.

### The draw, step by step

1. At the end of each tenday, ticket sales stop and the lamps dim. An announcer turns the three drums, one at a time, and each drops one stone numbered 0 to 9. At the table, the DM rolls **one d10 for each drum**. A 0 on the d10 is 0 (not 10).
2. The three results, in the order rolled, are the **winning number**.
3. Every winning ticket is paid in full at the cashier's cage. Losing tickets are worthless.

### Payouts

| Ticket | You win when… | Pays |
|---|---|---|
| **Exact Order** | All three digits match, in order | 600:1 |
| **Any Order** | All three digits match, in any order | 100:1 |

## Dice Examples

**Tickets:** Mirela (PC) buys a **1 gp Exact Order ticket on 4-1-7**. Thokk (NPC) buys a **1 gp Any Order ticket on 3-8-5**. "Fat" Oswin (NPC) buys a **1 sp Exact Order ticket on 9-9-9**.

**Tenday 1**
- Draw: **4**, **7**, **1**. The winning number is **4-7-1**.
- Mirela has the right digits but in the wrong order, and her ticket needs exact order. She **loses**.
- Thokk and Oswin **lose**.

**Tenday 2**
- Draw: **5**, **3**, **8**. The winning number is **5-3-8**.
- Thokk's Any Order ticket is 3-8-5: the same three digits in a different order. **He wins 100:1: 100 gp.**
- Mirela and Oswin **lose**.

**Tenday 3**
- Draw: **0**, **2**, **6**. Nobody wins. The House keeps every ticket sold.

**Edge case: Mirela wins with Exact Order**
- Draw: **4**, **1**, **7**. The winning number is **4-1-7**, exactly matching her ticket.
- **She wins 600:1: 600 gp** on a 1 gp ticket. The House pays it in public, to sell more tickets.

## Prizes & Stakes

| Ticket price | Exact Order pays | Any Order pays |
|---|---|---|
| 1 sp | 60 gp | 10 gp |
| 1 gp | 600 gp | 100 gp |
| 1 pp | 6,000 gp | 1,000 gp |

Every ticket is separate. Everyone holding a winning ticket is paid in full.

## Odds

| Ticket | Chance to win | Pays | House edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact Order | 0.1% (1 in 1,000) | 600:1 | 39.9% |
| Any Order | 0.6% (6 in 1,000) | 100:1 | 39.4% |

**This is the worst bet in the building and the House's best earner.** Players buy tickets for the dream, not the odds.

## Rulings & Cheating

### Fair Play: what's allowed

- Any digits, up to 10 tickets per draw.
- Lucky numbers, birthdays and digits "seen in a dream".

### What counts as cheating

- **Any spell** that affects the drums, such as *unseen servant*.
- Forged or altered tickets, or bribing the announcer.

### How to cheat (and how you get caught)

Each cheat is one **contest against the Floor Watcher's Wisdom (Perception) +5**. **Ties go to the Watcher.**

| Cheat | Who can do it | Roll vs the Watcher | If it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| After the draw, forge a ticket and its seal, or alter your own, and cash it at the cage | Player, proficient with a forgery kit | Dex (forgery kit) | The cashier pays the forged ticket |
| Bribe the announcer to palm a stone and drop it in place of the real one (100 gp) | Anyone | Cha (Persuasion) | Choose one digit of this draw |
| Hidden spell (*unseen servant* in the drum) | A caster in the crowd | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | As a drum turns, the invisible servant feels for the stone you chose and pushes it out. Choose that digit of the draw |

### Caught cheating: penalties

The Fates' Draw is played against the House. Penalties apply to the cheater **and** to any accomplice who is caught. For a forged ticket, "five times the bet" means five times the prize the forger tried to claim. The cap goes by the ticket's price: 1 sp uses the Silver Table's, 1 gp the Gold Table's and 1 pp the Platinum Salon's.

![[Cheating Penalties#Casino]]

### Cheating dice example

Quill (PC) buys a 1 gp Exact Order ticket on 3-8-5. The draw is 5-3-8, so he alters his ticket to read 5-3-8 and tries to claim the 600 gp prize.
- Quill: forgery kit **9** + 7 = **16**. The Watcher: **15** + 5 = **20**. **Caught.**
- His ticket and the night's winnings are taken, his portrait goes in the House's file, and he's banned for a month.
- He owes five times the prize he tried to claim, but the Gold Table cap is 250 gp, so he owes **250 gp** or a favour.

## Hooks / NPCs

- **A widow** knows her husband's last ticket won. He played 7-2-9 in Exact Order, at 1 gp, every draw for thirty years, and she buried his last ticket with him, in his coat pocket, for luck. Last night the drums dropped 7, 2, 9, which pays 600 gp. No ticket, no prize. She asks the party to help her dig him up. His brother says it's a sin, and that he'll tell the city watch.
- **A besotted poet** heard Lady Fortune, the masked announcer, call the numbers last tenday and fell in love with the voice. He's spending his rent on tickets to hear it again, and he has started following the staff home. Only the House knows that a different person wears the mask every tenday. Last tenday's was a shy half-orc cellar hand. The party can tell him, stop him or set up a meeting.
