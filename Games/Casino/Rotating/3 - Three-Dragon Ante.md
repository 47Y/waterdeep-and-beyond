# Three-Dragon Ante

> **Pitch (read aloud):** *Painted dragon cards fan across the table: red, blue, green, white, black, and the gleaming metallics. "Three gambits," the dealer says, "and the hoard goes to the cleverest." Coins clink into the center as the first cards fall.*

**Description:** A card game of painted dragons, played for real gold. Each **gambit** (one round of cards) is settled with an **Intelligence check**. Players proficient with a **three-dragon ante set** add their proficiency bonus. The highest roll takes the gambit's pot. Once per hand, a player can call on a dragon for a stronger draw, at a price.

## How to Play

### A hand, step by step

A hand is **three gambits**. A House dealer runs the table, opens a fresh deck for each hand and takes the rake, and doesn't play.

1. **Ante.** At the start of each gambit, every player puts the same ante into the pot.
2. **Draw a Dragon (optional, once per hand).** Before anyone rolls, going clockwise from the dealer's left, each player says whether they're drawing a Dragon. A player who does rolls **with advantage** this gambit, including on any reroll. If they **don't** win this gambit, they pay **one extra ante** to the winner.
3. **Play the gambit.** The cards are for show: you don't need real cards at the table, and the DM describes them to fit the results. Every player makes an **Intelligence check**, adding their proficiency bonus if they're proficient with a three-dragon ante set.
4. **Win the gambit.** The highest total takes the pot, minus the House's **5% rake**.
   - **Ties for the highest:** only the tied players roll again.
5. **Repeat** for the second and third gambits. Then a new hand starts, and everyone can Draw a Dragon again.

### Playing alone against the House

A single player can play against a House dealer with the **same modifier** as the player.
- The player bets, and the two play a **best of three gambits** (Intelligence checks, ties roll again).
- **The player wins two gambits:** the bet pays **9:10**.
- **The dealer wins two gambits:** the player loses the bet.
- Draw a Dragon isn't used in this version.

## Dice Examples

**At the table:** Mirela (PC, Int +1, proficient with the set +2 = **+3**), Thokk (NPC, Int +0, not proficient, **+0**) and Quill (PC, Int +2, not proficient, **+2**). The ante is **5 gp** per gambit. Each pot is **15 gp**; the rake is 7 sp 5 cp, so the winner takes **14 gp 2 sp 5 cp**.

**Gambit 1**
- Mirela **12** + 3 = **15**. Thokk **14** + 0 = **14**. Quill **7** + 2 = **9**.
- **Mirela wins** 14 gp 2 sp 5 cp.

**Gambit 2**
- Thokk declares **Draw a Dragon** and rolls with advantage: **4** and **18**, keeps 18. **18** + 0 = **18**.
- Mirela **9** + 3 = **12**. Quill **17** + 2 = **19**.
- **Quill wins** 14 gp 2 sp 5 cp. Thokk drew a Dragon and lost, so he pays Quill **one extra ante (5 gp)**.

**Gambit 3**
- Mirela **15** + 3 = **18**. Thokk **10** + 0 = **10**. Quill **16** + 2 = **18**.
- **Tie.** Mirela and Quill roll again: Mirela **11** + 3 = **14**, Quill **5** + 2 = **7**. **Mirela wins.**

**Edge case: playing alone against the House**
- Thokk (+0) bets **10 gp** against a +0 dealer.
- Gambit 1: Thokk **13**, dealer **8**. Thokk leads.
- Gambit 2: Thokk **6**, dealer **11**. One each.
- Gambit 3: Thokk **17**, dealer **14**. **Thokk wins two gambits** and the bet pays 9:10: **9 gp**.

## Odds

**Chance to win one gambit (equal players):**

| Players at the table | Normal roll | Drawing a Dragon (advantage) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 50% | 67.5% |
| 3 | 33% | 51% |
| 4 | 25% | 41% |

- **When to Draw a Dragon:** it pays if your modifier is better than your rivals', and it's close to even if you're level. If you're behind, the extra ante costs more than the advantage brings in.
- **Each +1 of modifier** over an equal rival adds about 5% to a two-player gambit.
- **Against the House dealer** (equal modifier), the player wins 50% of matches. Paying 9:10 gives the House a **5% edge**.
- **Between players,** the House's 5% rake means each player loses 5% of their antes on average.

## Rulings & Cheating

### Fair Play: what's allowed

- Drawing a Dragon once per hand.
- Proficiency with a three-dragon ante set.
- Table talk, boasting and dragon impressions.

### What counts as cheating

- **Any spell**, Bardic Inspiration or *guidance* on the check.
- Marked cards, a stacked deck, or a partner reading other players' cards.

### How to cheat (and how you get caught)

Each cheat is one **contest against the Floor Watcher's Wisdom (Perception) +5**. **Ties go to the Watcher.** Other players can also try to spot you.

| Cheat | Who can do it | Roll vs the Watcher | If it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nick the best cards with your thumbnail as you hold them in the first gambit, so that you can read them when they come round again | Player | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | Advantage on the other two gambits this hand. The next hand has a fresh deck |
| Bribe the dealer to stack the deck (Silver Table 1 gp, Gold Table 10 gp, Platinum Salon 100 gp) | Anyone | Cha (Persuasion) | +5 to every gambit this hand |
| Stand behind the other players, read their cards and signal them to the player | A helper in the crowd | Cha (Deception) to look casual | +2 to every gambit this hand |
| Hidden *guidance*, by touch: on yourself, or a friend's pat on the shoulder | The player or a friend | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | +1d4 to one gambit |

### Caught cheating: penalties

Three-Dragon Ante is played at a House table, so cheating at it is cheating the House. Penalties apply to the cheater **and** to any accomplice who is caught. Every pot the cheater won this hand goes back to the other players.

![[Cheating Penalties#Casino]]

### Cheating dice example

Quill (PC) nicks the best cards with his thumbnail in the first gambit of a hand at the Gold Table, with a 20 gp ante.
- Quill: Sleight of Hand **5** + 7 = **12**. The Watcher: **11** + 5 = **16**. **Caught.**
- His pots this hand go back to the other players. His chips and the night's winnings are taken, his portrait goes in the House's file, he's banned for a month, and he owes **100 gp** (five times the 20 gp ante) or a favour.

## Hooks / NPCs

- **"Lady Tiamat"**, a regular in five colored rings, plays one stranger at a time, for one stake. Beat her at best of three gambits and she'll truthfully answer any one question. Lose and you owe her one errand, with no questions asked. The last three who lost left the city within the week, and they won't say why. Some say she's more than a card player, and tonight she has turned to look at a party member.
- **A collector** pays 500 gp for a first-printing Dracolich card, and she's sure one got mixed in with the House's fresh decks. She plays whenever the table runs, just to see the cards. Tonight she sees it, in the hand of the party member beside her. It's House property and not for sale. She slides 500 gp across the table and whispers, "Put it up your sleeve."
