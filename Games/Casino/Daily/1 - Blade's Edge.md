# Blade's Edge

> **Pitch (read aloud):** *Two daggers are driven into the felt between you and the dealer. One d20 each, the House adds 1 to its own, and the higher number wins. When the numbers tie, the dealer pulls the daggers free and asks: "Walk away, or go to war?"*

**Description:** The simplest game in the casino. The player and the dealer each roll a d20, the dealer adds 1, and the higher number wins. A tie is the only time the player makes a choice: give up half the bet, or double it and roll again ("go to war"). It's quick and tense.

## How to Play

1. **Bet.** Place your bet.
2. **Roll.** You and the dealer each roll a **d20**, and the dealer **adds 1** to their roll. With more than one player at the table, the dealer rolls separately against each of them.
   - **Your number is higher:** you win 1:1.
   - **The dealer's number is higher:** you lose your bet.
   - **The numbers tie:** go to step 3.
3. **On a tie, choose:**
   - **Surrender:** you get half your bet back and lose the other half.
   - **Go to war:** add a second bet equal to the first, and both of you roll a **d20** again. The dealer adds 1 again.
     - **You win the war:** your second bet wins 1:1, and your first bet comes back (no win, no loss). You gain **one bet's worth**.
     - **The dealer wins the war:** you lose **both** bets.
     - **Another tie:** the House pays a bonus: you win **two bets' worth** and keep both bets.

**Surrendering loses slightly less on average,** but only a war can win you money.

## Dice Examples

**At the Gold Table:** Thokk (NPC) plays **10 gp** a hand.

**Hand 1**
- Thokk rolls **14**. The dealer rolls 9 + 1 = **10**. Thokk wins **10 gp**.

**Hand 2**
- Thokk rolls **6**. The dealer rolls 17 + 1 = **18**. Thokk **loses** 10 gp.

**Hand 3: a tie, and war**
- Thokk rolls **11**. The dealer rolls 10 + 1 = **11**. **Tie.**
- Thokk goes to war and adds another **10 gp** (20 gp on the table).
- War roll: Thokk **18**, dealer 4 + 1 = **5**. **Thokk wins the war.**
- His second 10 gp wins 1:1 and his first 10 gp comes back. He gains **10 gp**.

**Edge case: a lost war, then a surrender**
- Thokk rolls **3**; the dealer rolls 2 + 1 = **3**. He goes to war (20 gp on the table). War roll: Thokk **7**, dealer 15 + 1 = **16**. He **loses 20 gp**.
- Next hand: Thokk **12**, dealer 11 + 1 = **12**, another tie. Shaken, Thokk surrenders: he gets **5 gp** back and loses 5 gp.

**Edge case: a tie in the war**
- War roll: Thokk **9**, dealer 8 + 1 = **9**. The House pays the bonus. With 20 gp on the table, Thokk keeps his 20 gp and wins **20 gp** more.

**Edge case: both roll a 20**
- Thokk rolls **20**. The dealer rolls 20 + 1 = **21**. The dealer's number is higher, so Thokk **loses** 10 gp.

## Odds

| Result of one hand | Chance |
|---|---|
| Player wins | 42.75% |
| Dealer wins | 52.5% |
| Tie (war or surrender) | 4.75% |

| Choice on a tie | Average loss per tie |
|---|---|
| Go to war | 52.75% of one bet |
| Surrender | 50% of one bet |

**House edge: 12.1%** if the player always surrenders; **12.3%** if they always go to war.

## Rulings & Cheating

### Fair Play: what's allowed

- Choosing war or surrender on any tie.
- Calling the roll "for the blade" or kissing the d20.

### What counts as cheating

- **Any spell** that affects the dice.
- Loaded d20s, rolling "too softly" so the die barely turns, or changing your bet after the dealer rolls.

### How to cheat (and how you get caught)

Each cheat is one **contest against the Floor Watcher's Wisdom (Perception) +5**. **Ties go to the Watcher.**

| Cheat | Who can do it | Roll vs the Watcher | If it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft roll: set the d20 high face up and let it barely turn | Player | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | Roll your d20 twice this hand and keep the better |
| Swap the House d20 for your own loaded one | Player or a helper | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | Your d20 rolls with advantage for 10 hands, until the House changes the dice |
| Slide an extra chip onto your bet after the rolls are seen, while a friend distracts the dealer | Player | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | A winning bet pays as if you'd bet double |
| Hidden spell (*unseen servant* flip) | A caster nearby | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | The invisible servant flips the dealer's d20 to a 1 as it settles |

### Caught cheating: penalties

Blade's Edge is played against the House. Penalties apply to the cheater **and** to any accomplice who is caught.

### Cheating dice example

Quill (PC) and Mirela (PC) work together at the Gold Table. Quill bets 30 gp; Mirela quietly casts *unseen servant* to flip the dealer's die.
- Mirela: Deception **17** + 7 = **24**. The Watcher: **19** + 5 = **24**. **A tie goes to the Watcher. Caught.**
- Both lose their chips and the night's winnings, go in the House's file, and are banned for a month.
- Quill owes **150 gp** (five times his 30 gp bet). Mirela, the accomplice, faces the same penalties; the House sets her debt at the same 150 gp, or a favour from each of them.

## Hooks / NPCs

- **Two nobles**, forbidden by their families to draw steel, are fighting their duel at Blade's Edge instead. Each hires a champion to play ten hands against the dealer at 10 gp a hand, and the champion who ends up further ahead wins the quarrel. Surrendering a tie counts as running from the field, and loses the duel on the spot. Each noble covers their champion's losses and pays 50 gp for a win, and both of them have noticed the party.
- **A retired duelist** has gone to war on every tie for thirty years, and calls surrender a coward's way out. Tonight she's betting 50 gp of the money meant for her daughter's forge, with her daughter standing behind her chair. When the tie comes, she turns to the nearest party member and says, "Surrender or war? I'll do what you say, and I'll blame you for the rest of my days."
