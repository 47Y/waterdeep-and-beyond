# Proposition Betting

> **Pitch (read aloud):** *At a mahogany desk under a chalkboard covered in names and numbers, a bespectacled gnome licks her pencil. "Arena, tavern, card table, or the weather. Name it, and I'll give you a price."*

**Description:** The House bookie's desk. Players can bet on almost anything happening in the building (an arena bout, a tavern contest, a Three-Dragon Ante gambit) or even outside it. The bookie works out the chance of each outcome and offers **fixed odds**: the payout is set when the bet is made. Unlike [[Spectator Betting]], it doesn't matter how many other people bet.

## How to Play

### Making a bet, step by step

1. **Name the proposition.** For example: "Thokk wins King of the Drunks", "Mirela wins her next Three-Dragon Ante gambit", or "the Grand Derby favourite loses".
2. **The bookie sets the price.** The DM estimates the chance it happens, using that game's **Odds** section, and reads the payout from the table below.
3. **Bet.** The price is locked in.
4. **Settle.** When the event is over, a winning bet is paid at the locked price. A losing bet is gone.

### The bookie's prices

The bookie pays **0.9 ÷ chance** in total (including the bet). That builds in the House's 10% cut.

| Chance it happens | Total back per 1 gp bet | Pays |
|---|---|---|
| 90% or more | No bet offered | — |
| 75% | 1 gp 2 sp | 1:5 |
| 60% | 1 gp 5 sp | 1:2 |
| 50% | 1 gp 8 sp | 4:5 |
| 40% | 2 gp 2 sp 5 cp | 5:4 |
| 33% | 2 gp 7 sp | 17:10 |
| 25% | 3 gp 6 sp | 13:5 |
| 20% | 4 gp 5 sp | 7:2 |
| 12.5% | 7 gp | 6:1 |
| 10% | 9 gp | 8:1 |
| 5% | 18 gp | 17:1 |

For a chance between two rows, use the **lower** payout.

### House limits

- **Maximum bet:** the bookie follows the same table limits as the casino (see below).
- **No bets on yourself to lose.** Contestants may bet only on themselves to win.
- **Cancelled events:** all bets are refunded.

## Dice Examples

**At the desk:** Quill (PC) wants to bet on the party.

**Bet 1: a Three-Dragon Ante gambit**
- Mirela (+3) plays Thokk (+0), one on one. The Three-Dragon Ante odds give about **5% per +1**, so Mirela wins the gambit about **65%** of the time.
- That's between the 75% and 60% rows, so the bookie uses the lower payout: **1:2** (1 gp 5 sp back per 1 gp).
- Quill bets **10 gp on Mirela**. She wins. He collects **15 gp** (5 gp profit).

**Bet 2: a long shot**
- In a four-entrant lizard race where the DM judges each racer equal, the chance of any one winning is **25%**: **13:5**.
- Quill bets **5 gp** on Vessa's lizard. It loses. He's down 5 gp.

**Bet 3: an outside proposition**
- "It will rain on the day of the Grand Derby." The DM decides the chance is about **33%**: **17:10**.
- Quill bets **10 gp**. It rains. He collects **27 gp** (17 gp profit).

**Edge case: a sure thing**
- Quill asks for a price on "the sun rises tomorrow". The chance is over 90%, so the bookie laughs and **refuses the bet**.

## Odds

**House edge: 10% on every bet** (if the bookie's estimate is right).

**Better information beats the bookie.** A PC who knows something the bookie doesn't (a sick lizard, a bribed fighter, a secret proficiency) can find bets with a real edge. That's the whole point of the desk, and the bookie knows it.

## Rulings & Cheating

### Fair Play: what's allowed

- Betting on anything the bookie will price.
- Researching contestants, watching training sessions and asking around.

### What counts as cheating

- **Fixing the outcome:** bribing, drugging or threatening a contestant, or betting on yourself to lose.
- **Past posting:** betting after the result is already known.
- Forged betting slips.

### How to cheat (and how you get caught)

Each cheat is one **contest against the Floor Watcher's Wisdom (Perception) +5**. **Ties go to the Watcher.**

| Cheat | Who can do it | Roll vs the Watcher | If it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Past posting (a runner brings the result early) | Player with a fast helper | Cha (Deception) to look calm at the desk | Bet on a result that's already happened |
| Forged slip | Player, proficient with a forgery kit | Dex (forgery kit) | Cash a slip at better odds than you got |
| Fix the event | Player | Use the event's own cheat rules | The event's result is fixed |

**Fixing a public event** is cheating the public. The fixer faces **that event's** penalties as well as the House's.

### Cheating dice example

Quill bets 20 gp on a pit fight that his friend has just seen end.
- Quill: Deception **8** + 5 = **13**. The Watcher: **14** + 5 = **19**. **Caught.**
- He's confiscated, filed and banned for a month, and owes **100 gp** (five times his 20 gp bet) or a favour.

- **A huge bet** has been placed on a champion to lose. Fennwick wants to know who placed it, and why.
