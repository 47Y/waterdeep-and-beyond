# Bones of Fortune

> **Pitch (read aloud):** *Five carved bones rattle in a silver cup and slam down on black velvet. The cup lifts. "Full house!" the dealer calls, and the table erupts. One throw, five dice, and a hand of fortune.*

**Description:** A game of matching dice. The player throws **five dice once**, and the result is called a hand: pairs, three of a kind, runs and so on. Good hands pay; weak hands lose. A side bet called the **Hydra** pays on the most common hand, exactly one pair.

## How to Play

### A throw, step by step

1. **Bet.** Place your main bet. You may also place a **Hydra** side bet.
2. **Throw.** Players take turns with the one silver cup. Shake **five d6** in it, slam it upside down on the velvet and lift it. There are no rerolls.
3. **Read the hand** using the table below. Only the best hand counts.
4. **Pay out.**

### Main bet

| Hand | Example | Pays |
|---|---|---|
| **Five of a kind** | 4, 4, 4, 4, 4 | 100:1 |
| **Four of a kind** | 6, 6, 6, 6, 2 | 12:1 |
| **Full house** (three of a kind plus a pair) | 5, 5, 5, 2, 2 | 5:1 |
| **Straight**, a run of five (1-2-3-4-5 or 2-3-4-5-6) | 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 | 4:1 |
| **Three of a kind** | 3, 3, 3, 5, 1 | 1:2 (win half your bet) |
| Two pair, one pair, or nothing (no matches and no straight) | 4, 4, 1, 1, 6 | Lose |

### The Hydra side bet

Wins **1:1** if the hand is **exactly one pair** and the other three dice are all different (such as 2, 4, 4, 6, 1). Two pair, three of a kind or anything better **loses** the Hydra.

## Dice Examples

**At the table:** Mirela (PC) bets **2 gp** on the main bet and **2 gp** on the Hydra each throw.

**Throw 1**
- Dice: **3, 3, 3, 5, 1**. Three of a kind.
- Main bet pays 1:2: **1 gp**. Hydra: not exactly one pair, so it **loses** 2 gp.

**Throw 2**
- Dice: **2, 4, 4, 6, 1**. One pair (the 4s), and the other three are all different.
- Main bet **loses** 2 gp. Hydra wins 1:1: **2 gp**.

**Throw 3**
- Dice: **2, 3, 4, 5, 6**. A straight.
- Main bet pays 4:1: **8 gp**. Hydra **loses** 2 gp.

**Edge case: a full house kills the Hydra**
- Dice: **5, 5, 2, 2, 2**. A full house: there's a pair, but also three of a kind.
- Main bet pays 5:1: **10 gp**. Hydra **loses**, because the hand is more than exactly one pair.

## Rulings & Cheating

### Fair Play: what's allowed

- Any bet within the table limits.
- Blowing on the cup, chanting and calling your hand before it lands.

### What counts as cheating

- **Any spell** that affects the dice.
- Loaded dice, or turning a die under the cup as you lift it.

### How to cheat (and how you get caught)

Each cheat is one **contest against the Floor Watcher's Wisdom (Perception) +5**. **Ties go to the Watcher.**

| Cheat | Who can do it | Roll vs the Watcher | If it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tilt the cup a finger's width to peek, then turn one die with a fingertip as you lift it | Player | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | Set one die to the face of your choice |
| Swap two of the House dice for your own loaded ones | Player or a helper | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | For 10 throws, until the House changes the dice, each of those two dice that rolls a 5 counts as a 6 |
| Hidden spell (*unseen servant* nudge) | A caster nearby | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | As the cup lifts, the invisible servant turns one die to a face of your choice |

### Caught cheating: penalties

Bones of Fortune is played against the House. Penalties apply to the cheater **and** to any accomplice who is caught.

### Cheating dice example

Quill (PC) bets 10 gp and slams the cup down. He tilts it a finger's width and sees **6, 6, 6, 6, 3**. One die from five of a kind, he turns the 3 to a 6 with a fingertip as he lifts the cup.
- Quill: Sleight of Hand **13** + 7 = **20**. The Watcher: **17** + 5 = **22**. **Caught.**
- His chips and the night's winnings are taken, his portrait goes in the House's file, he's banned for a month, and he owes **50 gp** (five times his 10 gp bet) or a favour.

## Hooks / NPCs

- **A ruined wine merchant** has heard that the silver cup throws five of a kind for anyone who has truly lost everything, so he's been giving away what he has left. His shop went first, then his horse, and tonight his boots. All he has now is a silver ring and a 5 gp bet, which would pay 500 gp at a chance of 1 in 1,296. He asks the party to take the ring, so that it counts. His daughter asks them to take it and give it to her.
- **A dwarven bone-carver** sells "lucky" dice at 30 gp a set, all loaded the same way, and the party has just watched the Watcher's nephew buy some. The young fool plans to swap them in tonight, in front of his uncle. The carver offers the party 30 gp to say nothing. They could take the money, warn the nephew or tell his uncle.
