# The Deadly Divination

> **Pitch (read aloud):** *Six crystal vials glow on a black table laid out like an altar, identical in every way. Five hold sweet cordial. One holds the Bitter Fate. The hooded Diviner lifts the cloth, bows, and gestures: "Drink, and let fate decide."*

**Description:** A test of nerve against the House. Six vials sit on the table, and one of them is the foul **Bitter Fate**. The player drinks them one at a time, and each sweet vial raises their winnings. Stop whenever you like and collect. Drink the Bitter Fate and you lose your bet. Once per game, you can examine a vial before you drink it, so a trained healer has a real edge here. The Diviner runs the table and never drinks.

## How to Play

### Setting up

1. **Bet.** Place your bet.
2. **The vials come out,** numbered 1 to 6. A House attendant fills them in the back room, so the Diviner doesn't know which is which either. The DM secretly rolls a **d6**. That vial (1–6) holds the Bitter Fate.

### Your turn, step by step

Repeat these steps until the game ends. You must drink at least one vial, or you lose your bet.

1. **Choose one:**
   - **Drink** any vial.
   - **Stop** and collect your winnings. You can do this once you've drunk at least one sweet vial.
   - **Examine** a vial (see below).
2. **If you drink sweet cordial:** the Diviner stacks your new winnings in front of you. Go back to step 1.
3. **If you drink the Bitter Fate:** you lose your bet and any winnings so far, and you're **poisoned for 10 minutes** (it tastes of burnt hair and vinegar).

| Sweet vials drunk | You win | On a 10 gp bet |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1:10 | 1 gp |
| 2 | 2:5 | 4 gp |
| 3 | 4:5 | 8 gp |
| 4 | 8:5 | 16 gp |
| 5 | 4:1 | 40 gp |

After five sweet vials, the last one must be the Bitter Fate. The game ends and you collect. Nobody ever drinks the last vial.

### Examining a vial

**Once per game**, pick one vial and make a **DC 20 Wisdom (Medicine)** check, or an Intelligence check with **alchemist's supplies** or a **poisoner's kit**.
- **Success:** the DM tells you privately, and truthfully, whether that vial is the Bitter Fate.
- **Failure:** you learn nothing.

**An examine is your last move.** After it, you can only drink the vial you examined, or stop and collect. You can't touch the others, even if you now know they're safe.

If you're poisoned, you have disadvantage on the check.

## Dice Examples

**At the table:** Mirela (PC, Medicine +0) bets **10 gp**.

**Game 1**
- The DM rolls **5**: the Bitter Fate is in **vial 5**.
- Mirela drinks vial 2: sweet. Winnings: **1 gp**.
- She drinks vial 3: sweet. Winnings: **4 gp**.
- She stops, and **wins 4 gp**.

**Game 2**
- The DM rolls **2**: the Bitter Fate is in **vial 2**.
- Mirela drinks vials 6, 1 and 4: all sweet. Winnings: **8 gp**.
- She examines vial 2: Medicine **20** + 0 = **20**. Success: the DM tells her privately that it's the Bitter Fate.
- She now knows vials 3 and 5 are safe, but an examine is her last move. She stops, and **wins 8 gp**.

**Game 3**
- The DM rolls **1**: the Bitter Fate is in **vial 1**.
- Mirela drinks vial 3: sweet. Winnings: **1 gp**.
- She drinks vial 1. **The Bitter Fate!** She **loses** her 10 gp bet and the 1 gp, and is poisoned for 10 minutes.

**Edge case: a healer at the table**
- A cleric with **Medicine +9** bets 10 gp and drinks four sweet vials. Winnings: **16 gp**. Vials 4 and 6 are left.
- They examine vial 4: Medicine **11** + 9 = **20**. Success: it's safe.
- They drink it. Five sweet vials, so they **win 40 gp** and the game ends. The House starts to notice.

## Odds

| Stop after | Chance to get this far | House edge |
|---|---|---|
| 1 sweet vial | 83.3% | 8.3% |
| 2 sweet vials | 66.7% | 6.7% |
| 3 sweet vials | 50% | 10% |
| 4 sweet vials | 33.3% | 13.3% |
| 5 sweet vials | 16.7% | 16.7% |

**Without an examine, your best plan is to stop after two sweet vials.** Chasing the big prizes costs you more.

**With an examine, played at its best:**

| Examine bonus | Examine succeeds | Best plan | House edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Never examines | — | Stop after two | 6.7% |
| +0 | 5% | Drink two, then examine | 5.7% |
| +2 | 15% | Drink two, then examine | 3.7% |
| +5 | 30% | Drink two, then examine | 0.7% |
| +9 | 50% | Drink four, then examine | **−6.7% (the player has the edge)** |
| +14 | 75% | Drink four, then examine | **−16.7% (the player has the edge)** |

**The House watches its healers.** Anyone who keeps winning gets a polite word, and the table is closed to them for a tenday.

## Rulings & Cheating

### Fair Play: what's allowed

- One examine per game, using Medicine, alchemist's supplies or a poisoner's kit.
- Sniffing, swirling and holding vials up to the light. (That's part of the examine.)
- Immunity to the poisoned condition: it doesn't change who wins, but it spares the drinker.

### What counts as cheating

- **Any spell or magic** that helps you find the Bitter Fate, such as *detect poison and disease*, *guidance* or Bardic Inspiration.
- Examining more than once, swapping a vial, or bribing whoever fills them.

### How to cheat (and how you get caught)

Each cheat is one **contest against the Floor Watcher's Wisdom (Perception) +5**. **Ties go to the Watcher.**

| Cheat | Who can do it | Roll vs the Watcher | If it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| While the Diviner stacks your winnings and every eye is on the chips, lift and sniff a vial | Player | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | Examine that vial. It doesn't count as your examine, so your game carries on |
| While the Diviner stacks your winnings and every eye is on the chips, swap a vial for a sweet one from your sleeve | Player | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | That vial is safe to drink. If the one you took was the Bitter Fate, every vial left is sweet, though you won't know it |
| Hidden spell (*detect poison and disease*) | A caster within 30 ft | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | Know which vial is the Bitter Fate for the spell's 10 minutes, about three games |
| Hidden *guidance*, by touch: on yourself, or a friend's pat on the shoulder | The player or a friend | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | +1d4 on your examine |
| Bardic Inspiration, called out as a word of encouragement | A bard within 60 ft | Cha (Deception) to pass it off as a friend's cheer | The player adds the die to their examine |
| Bribe the attendant who fills the vials (Silver Table 1 gp, Gold Table 10 gp, Platinum Salon 100 gp) | Anyone | Cha (Persuasion) | For the next game, the attendant lets you know which vial is the Bitter Fate |

### Caught cheating: penalties

The Deadly Divination is played against the House. Penalties apply to the cheater **and** to any accomplice who is caught.

### Cheating dice example

Quill (PC) bets 20 gp and drinks a sweet vial. While every eye is on the Diviner stacking his winnings, he tries to swap a vial for a sweet one from his sleeve.
- Quill: Sleight of Hand **12** + 7 = **19**. The Watcher: **16** + 5 = **21**. **Caught.**
- His chips and the night's winnings are taken, his portrait goes in the House's file, he's banned for a month, and he owes **100 gp** (five times his 20 gp bet) or a favour.

## Hooks / NPCs

- **An apothecary** has won too often here, and the table is closed to her for a tenday. Her shop's rent is due in three days. She offers to stake a party member 100 gp to play for her, and to split the winnings. Nothing in the rules forbids it, but the Diviner has noticed them talking, and the table can close to them too.
- **A merchant** has drunk until he hit the Bitter Fate three nights running, on purpose, and he looks more frightened each time. It tastes of burnt hair and vinegar, and so has his wine at home, for a month. He offers the party 100 gp to come to dinner and taste it, and to say nothing to his wife, who has already said how much she'd like to meet them.
