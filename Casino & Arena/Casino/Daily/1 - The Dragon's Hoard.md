# The Dragon's Hoard

> **Pitch (read aloud):** *A chest with glass sides stands in the middle of the floor, heaped with gold, and a chalkboard above it shows the total. Everyone who walks past stops to look. Five gold pieces, one turn of a brass handle, and all of it could be yours.*

**Description:** A jackpot that grows until someone wins it. Each turn costs **5 gp**, and that money goes onto the pile. Three dice tumble in a brass cage, and three zeroes, a 1 in 1,000 chance, wins **80% of the pile**. There are no small prizes. It needs no skill and no decisions.

## How to Play

### The setup

The glass chest holds **the pot**, which starts at **500 gp** of the House's money. An attendant runs the game and keeps the total on the chalkboard. Next to the chest is a **brass cage** with a handle, holding three ten-sided dice numbered 0 to 9. The House owns the dice, and they stay in the cage.

### A turn, step by step

Players line up and take one turn each. If you want another turn, go to the back of the line.

1. **Pay.** Give the attendant **5 gp**. It goes into the chest, and the attendant adds it to the chalkboard.
2. **Turn the handle.** The dice tumble and stop. At the table, roll **three d10**.
3. **Read the dice.**
   - **0, 0, 0:** **jackpot!** You win **80% of the pot**, including the 5 gp you just paid.
   - **Anything else:** nothing happens, and your 5 gp stays in the pot.

### After a jackpot

The House keeps the other 20% of the pot, puts **500 gp** of its own into the chest, and the game starts again.

The pot carries over from night to night until someone wins it.

## Dice Examples

**At the chest:** the chalkboard shows **1,240 gp**. Mirela (PC) and Thokk (NPC) are in line.

**Mirela's turn**
- She pays 5 gp. The pot is now **1,245 gp**.
- The dice show **3, 7, 0**. Nothing happens.

**Thokk's turn**
- He pays 5 gp. The pot is now **1,250 gp**.
- The dice show **0, 0, 4**. Two zeroes are worth nothing. Nothing happens.

**Mirela's next turn**
- She pays 5 gp. The pot is now **1,255 gp**.
- The dice show **0, 0, 0**. **Jackpot!** She wins 80% of 1,255 gp: **1,004 gp**.
- The House keeps 251 gp and puts 500 gp into the chest. The chalkboard now shows **500 gp**.

**Edge case: a win on the very next turn**
- Thokk pays 5 gp. The pot is now **505 gp**.
- The dice show **0, 0, 0**. He wins 80% of 505 gp: **404 gp**. That's the smallest jackpot there can be.

## Prizes & Stakes

**Every turn costs 5 gp.** You can't bet more or less, and the table limits don't apply here.

**The winner gets 80% of the pot.** A big win can be taken in platinum, because 10,000 gp in gold weighs 200 pounds.

## Odds

**Chance of a jackpot on any turn: 0.1%** (1 in 1,000). Every turn has the same chance, so the pot is never "due" to pay.

| Pots won by | Turn | Pot | Winner gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smallest possible | 1st | 505 gp | 404 gp |
| 1 pot in 10 | 106th | 1,030 gp | 824 gp |
| 1 pot in 4 | 288th | 1,940 gp | 1,552 gp |
| Half of all pots | 693rd | 3,965 gp | 3,172 gp |
| 3 pots in 4 | 1,386th | 7,430 gp | 5,944 gp |
| 9 pots in 10 | 2,302nd | 12,010 gp | 9,608 gp |

**The average pot** takes 1,000 turns and reaches 5,500 gp, and the average win is **4,400 gp**. There's no ceiling: about 1 pot in 7 passes 10,500 gp.

**House edge: 12%.** Over 1,000 turns, players pay in 5,000 gp and the winner gets about 4,400 gp back.

**When a turn is worth its price:** once the pot passes **6,250 gp**, which happens in about 1 pot in 3. A turn is a good bet from then on, and a rich party might stand in line all night. The House doesn't mind, because it's other players' money.

**The House's risk:** it loses money on a pot won before the 400th turn, about 1 pot in 3. The most it can lose on one pot is 399 gp.

## Rulings & Cheating

### Fair Play: what's allowed

- As many turns as you like, as long as you go to the back of the line each time.
- Pooling money with friends and agreeing to share a win.
- Blowing on the coins, and talking to the cage.

### What counts as cheating

- **Any spell** that affects the dice, such as *unseen servant*.
- Fake coins, easing the handle so that a die doesn't tumble, or bribing the attendant.

### How to cheat (and how you get caught)

Each cheat is one **contest against the Floor Watcher's Wisdom (Perception) +5**. **Ties go to the Watcher.**

| Cheat | Who can do it | Roll vs the Watcher | If it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hand over five lead slugs painted gold, in place of real coins | Player | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | This turn is free |
| When a die was left showing 0, ease the handle with a practised hand so that it barely moves | Player, right after that turn | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | That die stays on 0 this turn. Roll the other two |
| Bribe the attendant to drop your loaded die into the cage on your turns (10 gp) | Anyone | Cha (Persuasion) | For your next 10 turns, until the House checks the dice, a 9 on that die counts as a 0 |
| Hidden spell (*unseen servant* nudge) | A caster nearby | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | As the dice settle, the invisible servant turns one die to a face of your choice |

### Caught cheating: penalties

The Dragon's Hoard is played against the House. Penalties apply to the cheater **and** to any accomplice who is caught. Any jackpot won by cheating goes back into the pot.

![[Cheating Penalties#Casino]]

### Cheating dice example

The dice show **0, 4, 7** on Thokk's turn, and Quill (PC) is next in line. He pays his 5 gp and eases the handle, trying to keep that 0 where it is.
- Quill: Sleight of Hand **13** + 7 = **20**. The Watcher: **17** + 5 = **22**. **Caught.**
- His chips and the night's winnings are taken, his portrait goes in the House's file, he's banned for a month, and he owes **25 gp** (five times his 5 gp turn) or a favour. His 5 gp stays in the pot.

## Hooks / NPCs

- **Three merchants** have worked out that a turn is worth its price once the pot passes 6,250 gp, and tonight it has. They've hired every dock hand they could find, at 1 sp an hour plus 5 gp for each turn, to fill the whole line until someone wins, and hand the prize over. An old woman who has paid in 5 gp a night for years can't get near the cage, and she asks the party to get her a place. The merchants offer the party the same 1 sp an hour to join the line.
- **A sewing circle** of twelve has paid for one turn a week for nine years, sharing the cost and any prize. Tonight the youngest turned the handle and hit 0, 0, 0, for 6,000 gp. She says it's all hers, because the circle's purse was empty this week and she paid the 5 gp herself. The others say a promise is a promise. They agree on one thing: the party can judge it, and they'll all accept the verdict.
