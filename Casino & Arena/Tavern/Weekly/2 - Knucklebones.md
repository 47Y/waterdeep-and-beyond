# Knucklebones

> **Pitch (read aloud):** *Five little bones scattered on the table, and one big painted one: the Ogre. Toss the Ogre up, sweep up bones before it falls, and catch it. One bone, then two, then three... up to all five at once. Stop while you're ahead, or push on and risk the whole turn.*

**Description:** A weekly game of knucklebones. You toss the **Ogre** (the big painted bone) in the air, sweep up a number of small bones, and catch the Ogre before it lands. There are **five stages**: one bone, then two, up to all five. Each stage is harder. After every stage you clear, you choose: **stop** and keep your progress, or **go on** and risk losing everything you've cleared this turn. The first to clear all five stages wins.

## How to Play

### Setting up

- **One set of bones** for the table: five small bones and the Ogre. Players share it, passing it to the left after each turn.
- **One hand only.** The same hand tosses the Ogre, sweeps up the bones and catches the Ogre.
- The bones are scattered on the table again before every try.
- Everyone rolls a d20. The highest goes first, then play goes left. Everyone starts at stage 1.

### Your turn, step by step

1. **Try your current stage.** Make a **Dexterity (Sleight of Hand)** check:

   | Stage | Sweep up | DC |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | 1 bone | 10 |
   | 2 | 2 bones | 12 |
   | 3 | 3 bones | 14 |
   | 4 | 4 bones | 16 |
   | 5 | All 5 bones | 18 |

2. **Success:** you've cleared that stage. Choose:
   - **Stop:** pass the bones on. The stages you've cleared are safe, and next turn you start at the next stage.
   - **Go on:** try the next stage straight away.
3. **Failure** (you miss a bone, or the Ogre hits the table): your turn ends and you **lose every stage you cleared this turn**. Next turn, you start again from the stage you started on this turn.

### Winning

The first player to **clear stage 5** wins, straight away.

## Dice Examples

**Players:** Mirela (PC, Sleight of Hand +2) and Lark Thistlewhite (NPC, Sleight of Hand +5). Mirela goes first.

**Turn 1**
- **Mirela:** Stage 1 (DC 10): **13** + 2 = **15**. ✓ She goes on. Stage 2 (DC 12): **9** + 2 = **11**. ✗ She loses stage 1, so next turn she starts at stage 1 again.
- **Lark:** Stage 1: **12** + 5 = **17**. ✓ He goes on. Stage 2: **8** + 5 = **13**. ✓ He stops. Next turn he starts at stage 3.

**Turn 2**
- **Mirela:** Stage 1: **14** + 2 = **16**. ✓ She stops. Next turn she starts at stage 2.
- **Lark:** Stage 3 (DC 14): **10** + 5 = **15**. ✓ He goes on. Stage 4 (DC 16): **16** + 5 = **21**. ✓ He stops. Next turn he starts at stage 5.

**Turn 3**
- **Mirela** is far behind, so she pushes. Stage 2: **15** + 2 = **17**. ✓ Stage 3: **17** + 2 = **19**. ✓ Stage 4: **11** + 2 = **13**. ✗ She loses stages 2 and 3, and starts at stage 2 again next turn.
- **Lark:** Stage 5 (DC 18): **13** + 5 = **18**. ✓ **Lark wins!**

**Edge case: failing the first try of a turn**
- If Lark had rolled **12** + 5 = **17** on stage 5, he'd fail. He cleared nothing this turn, so he loses nothing: he tries stage 5 again next turn.

## Prizes & Stakes

- **Stake:** 1 sp per player. The winner takes it all, minus 1 cp for the House.
- **The House keeps:** the 1 cp and the drinks.

## Odds

**Chance to clear stages 1 and 5, then average turns to win, by how many stages you clear per turn before stopping:**

| Sleight of Hand | Clear stage 1 | Clear stage 5 | Stop after 1 | Stop after 2 | Stop after 3 | Never stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| +2 | 65% | 25% | 12.4 | 13.1 | 17.6 | 71.0 |
| +4 | 75% | 35% | 9.8 | 8.9 | 10.1 | 23.7 |
| +7 | 90% | 50% | 7.5 | 5.8 | 5.3 | 6.6 |

**Pushing too far is the way to lose.** Weak hands should stop after every stage, most players after two, and only the best hands after three. All five in one turn works just **1.4%** of the time at +2, **4.2%** at +4 and **15%** at +7.

**Going first** is a small edge: about **54%** between two equal players. A player who falls behind has a reason to push, and a good chance of paying for it.

## Rulings & Cheating

### Fair Play: what's allowed

- Any sweeping technique, one hand only.
- Stopping or going on, whenever you clear a stage.
- Your own set of bones, if the other players agree.

### What counts as cheating

- **Any spell or magic**, such as *mage hand*, *guidance* or Bardic Inspiration.
- Pine resin on your palm, or bumping the table.

### How to cheat (and how you get caught)

Each cheat is one **contest against the Floor Watcher's Wisdom (Perception) +5**. **Ties go to the Watcher.** The party can help, but each person who tries a cheat makes their own roll and risks being caught.

| Cheat | Who can do it | Roll vs the Watcher | If it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smear pine resin on your palm, so the bones stick | Player | Dex (Sleight of Hand) | +2 to every stage this game |
| Bump the table on a rival's toss, made to look like an accident | Anyone at the table | Cha (Deception) | The rival has disadvantage on that stage |
| Quietly cast *guidance* on yourself between tries | A player who can cast it | Cha (Deception) to disguise the casting. Subtle Spell gives advantage. | +1d4 on one stage |
| Bardic Inspiration, shouted as a cheer | A bard in the crowd, within 60 ft | Cha (Deception) to pass it off as ordinary crowd noise | The player adds the die to one stage |

### Cheating dice example

On Lark's stage 5, Mirela bumps the table.
- Mirela: Deception **7** + 7 = **14**. The Watcher: **12** + 5 = **17**. **Caught.**
- Mirela forfeits her stake, is banned from the bones table for 3 months, and is pinned to the notice board. Lark tries his stage 5 again, with no bump. To clear her name: 1 gp, 1 day serving, or wait 3 months.

## Hooks / NPCs

- **The tavern's Ogre** is real: a knucklebone from an actual ogre, painted over. A collector has offered a fortune for it. The barkeep won't sell.
- **Granny Pell** plays every tenday and has never lost. Everyone plays one-handed, but she has no choice: she lost the other hand to a real ogre.
